Table rules & strategy notes

Rainbet publishes eight decks, dealer stands on all 17s, a 3:2 natural payout, and the double and split actions. Its public rules do not say whether doubles are allowed after a split; the verifier describes card generation, not action eligibility.

After a hit, enter the card received to get updated advice. After you stand, after a non-busting double, or when your hand reaches 21, enter the dealer's hole card and required draws. The dealer must reach 17 or bust before the hand completes; a player bust ends without dealer draws. After a recommended split, use the split prompt to deal one new card to each hand before playing hand 1 to completion, then hand 2, and only then enter the dealer's cards. Split aces receive one card each and stand automatically. The experimental outcome-pattern estimate is separate from strategy and does not change move recommendations.

Strategy overview

The exact decision implementation runs on the advisor server and is not included in this public page. For eligible non-split hands against dealer 2–9, it compares hit, stand, and available double actions using eight-deck S17 rank probabilities with a replacement approximation. Pair decisions and hands against dealer 10 or Ace retain the published-rule basic-strategy baseline because some live-table rules are not published. Separate action-outcome estimates include dealer 10/A by assuming the dealer checks for blackjack before player actions; those percentages include the dealer-blackjack chance as a loss. They are assumptions, not guarantees or predictions, and actual strategy may differ if live rules differ.

Card ranks, dealer upcard, and selected rule toggles are sent to the app server for each move recommendation. Hand history, outcome, wager, and fictional-credit balance run locally for the pre-deal sizing estimate and are not sent for move calculations.

Moving the implementation to a server hides its source from ordinary page inspection, but repeated requests can still reveal recommendation behavior. It is not a guarantee against reverse engineering.

The advisor does not track cards across hands, make real-money wager recommendations, or place bets. Its current-hand model estimates win/push/loss chances for each available hit, stand, or double action with an 8-deck S17 replacement model. For dealer 10/A, it assumes a dealer check for blackjack before player actions and includes that chance as a loss. For eligible pairs against dealer 2–9, Split outcomes are per split hand, not the combined two-hand result; they use modeled follow-up play, omit resplitting, and assume no double after splitting when DAS is unknown. These percentages describe outcomes, not the chance an action is correct. DOUBLE uses one more card and twice the exposure. The recent-20 history model compares chronological out-of-sample multiclass Brier scores against an overall baseline and exposes an approximate paired lower bound. It is a separate pre-deal input: Auto may use it for a fictional amount only with at least 30 predictions and a positive lower bound; otherwise the baseline or recorded-return evidence remains. The stake is independent of the current cards and future action. First-action W/P/L rates provide context, while split history entries remain round-level results. History probabilities assume even-money W/L for sizing, and exact recorded net returns can cap the amount. This experimental gate is not proof of a durable advantage. The current-hand model still uses replacement assumptions and may not match live rules. Balance, session-loss, and up-to-4× exposure caps apply. Confirm current table rules and terms before relying on any reference.

Method references: NIST guidance on Wilson proportion intervals, Wizard of Odds blackjack expected values, card-removal effects, and proper scoring rules and Brier-score evaluation. Kelly sizing assumes a known return distribution and positive expected value; this practice model's approximate validation does not establish a durable edge.

Sources: Rainbet Blackjack rules and Rainbet terms.

Basic-strategy practice

Practice uses the same strategy rules as the advisor and prioritizes logged decisions that differed from those rules. Outcome-pattern estimates are separate experiments; they do not change recommendations or affect which actions are taught.

Practice uses retrieval and spaced review: correct answers return after 1, 3, 7, then 14 days; missed answers return after 10 minutes. These intervals are simple defaults, not a blackjack-specific or scientifically optimized schedule. Research reviews support practice testing and distributed practice in general learning: Dunlosky et al. (2013), Cepeda et al. (2006), and Roediger & Karpicke (2006).

Rainbet’s public Blackjack page shows selected bet amounts, payout multipliers, and dates, but not a representative bulk dataset of player/dealer cards and decisions. Those highlights are not used to train advice: they cannot establish which action is best, and selected high payouts are not predictive of future hands.

          Variables in Rainbet’s published sequence verifier

          Rainbet’s public Blackjack verifier documents the following inputs and constants for reproducing a completed hand. This describes the published verifier, not a way to know a future hand.

          • Server seed: secret HMAC key used to generate the sequence. Its hash can be committed before play; the original seed is needed for retrospective verification after it is revealed.
          • Client seed: public string combined with the round’s nonce.
          • Nonce: the round identifier/counter supplied to the verifier. Changing it changes the input used to derive the sequence for the same seeds.
          • Game label: the fixed string blackjack, which separates this game’s stream from other uses.
          • Step counter: starts at 0 and increments for each HMAC-SHA-512 digest. The message for each digest is clientSeed:nonce:blackjack:step.
          • Digest words: each 64-byte HMAC digest is read as sixteen unsigned 32-bit values, in order, as the random-number stream.
          • Shoe definition: ranks A, 2–9, T, J, Q, K in suits c, d, h, s, with eight copies of the 52-card deck (416 cards total). The published code’s deck order is part of reproducing the same result.
          • Draw parameters: draw 16 cards without replacement. For each draw, the range is the number of cards remaining. Rejection sampling discards values at or above 2^32 - (2^32 mod range), then selects value mod range and swaps that card into the next draw position.

          In the published sequence function, the wager amount and your hit/stand/double/split choices are not inputs to sequence generation. They affect how you play and settle the hand, not the seed-derived card stream. Previous hand results are not inputs either; the nonce is the round-specific value.

          This page does not collect seeds/nonces or reproduce sequences. Use Rainbet’s official verifier to check completed rounds. A revealed server seed can verify past outcomes; it does not reveal the next secret server seed.

          Important limits

          This is a basic-strategy reference, not a money-making or card-prediction system. Rainbet lists a maximum RTP of 99.60% for this game; the house still has an edge. Following basic strategy can reduce avoidable errors, but short-run losses and losing streaks remain normal.

          Rainbet’s verifier derives an eight-deck shoe from the seed pair and hand nonce; past hands do not deplete the next shoe. Rainbet’s current terms also prohibit card counting. This page does not connect to Rainbet, place bets, or attempt to predict hidden cards.

          Sources: Rainbet Originals Blackjack rules, Rainbet’s blackjack verifier, and Rainbet terms, section 4.6. Strategy is approximate because Rainbet’s public page does not state every rule detail.
